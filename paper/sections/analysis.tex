\paragraph{Failure cases.}
Table~\ref{tab:failures} lists four Tier~2 errors (maps in \texttt{results/stage8\_analysis}).
In \#1 the map stays low on the melt among dense components and peaks in the background: the defect is
not seen.
In \#2 the high score comes from the object--background boundary, not the tiny spot, so the near miss is
coincidental.
In \#3 and \#4 the board sits slightly off its usual position and the maximum falls on the background or
board edge.
The shared blind spot is position: a location-wise Gaussian flags the edges of a shifted object, which
masks small defects.
Over all test anomalies (seed~0) the Tier~2 map peaks inside the defect mask (dilated by 8\,px) for 25.3\% of
images (0\% for capsules), against 70.3\% for Tier~3.
The first fix to try is registering each image to a reference pose before scoring.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
H1 is only partly supported.
Capsules has the largest Tier~3 $-$ Tier~2 gap ($+0.196$ PR-AUC), but on macaroni2 Tier~2 is better
(0.544 vs.\ 0.492, both weak; chance is 0.375), and the multiple-instance group gap ($+0.095$) equals
the complex-structure gap.
H2 is partly supported: patch-level scoring beats pooling overall ($-0.029$ when pooled, beyond seed
std), but per defect size its recall gains are 0.01--0.03 (small $<$0.25\%: 0.770 vs.\ 0.742; large
$>$0.98\%: 0.983 vs.\ 0.969), within 1--2 seed std and not largest for small defects.

\paragraph{Practical impact.}
Of 10,000 items at a 1\% defect rate, Tier~2 with best-F2 misses 18 of 100 defects and sends 3,288
good items to re-inspection (2.4\% of flagged items are defective); Tier~3 misses about as many (18.5)
with about half the false alarms (1,722), roughly halving the inspectors' workload.
If only about 2\% of production can be re-inspected, Tier~2 needs the 99th-percentile threshold (186
false alarms) and misses 71 defects.
Labour versus escaped defects is the quality manager's choice; Tier~3 cuts the labour at no cost in
escaped defects.

\paragraph{Interpretability.}
In Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} Tier~2 flags the chewing gum by a wide margin ($s/\tau = 5.19$), but its
map peaks on the shadow above the object; Tier~3 peaks on the missing chunk.
For a missing pcb4 component both maps peak on the empty pad, so an engineer could act on either.
Mean pixel ROC-AUC (seed~0) is 0.925 for Tier~2 and 0.973 for Tier~3, so we would show engineers the Tier~3 map
and not trust the Tier~2 map, which hits the defect in only a quarter of cases.
