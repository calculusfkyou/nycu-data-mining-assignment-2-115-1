\paragraph{Pipeline.}
Tier~2 (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}) follows PaDiM \citep{defard2021padim}.
A frozen ImageNet-pretrained WideResNet-50 \citep{zagoruyko2016wide} produces layer2
($512 \times 28^2$), layer3 ($1024 \times 14^2$) and layer4 ($2048 \times 7^2$) maps; layer3 and layer4
are bilinearly upsampled to $28 \times 28$ and concatenated into a 3,584-d feature $f_{ij}(x)$ at each
of the 784 locations.
Because the features are redundant (\S\ref{sec:eda}), we keep a random subset of $d = 200$ channels,
drawn once per seed.
Layers and $d$ were chosen on val (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Anomaly score.}
At every location we fit a Gaussian $(\mu_{ij}, \Sigma_{ij})$ on train normals; the location score is
the Mahalanobis distance and the image score its maximum:
\begin{align}
  s_{ij}(x) &= \sqrt{(f_{ij} - \mu_{ij})^\top (\Sigma_{ij} + \epsilon I)^{-1} (f_{ij} - \mu_{ij})},
  \label{eq:sij} \\
  s(x) &= \max_{i,j}\, s_{ij}(x), \label{eq:sx}
\end{align}
with $\epsilon = 0.01$ to keep $\Sigma_{ij}$ invertible.
We take the maximum because the median defect covers 0.20\% of the image, about 1.6 of the 784
locations, so averaging would dilute it; the map $s_{ij}(x)$ also explains each alert
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\paragraph{Threshold and error costs.}
An alert is raised when $s(x) \ge \tau$, where $\tau$ maximises $F_2 = 5PR/(4P+R)$ on val, per object.
A false negative ships a defective product to a customer (returns, possible safety risk); a false
positive sends a good product to manual re-inspection (labour).
Because false negatives cost more, $F_2$, which weights recall above precision, suppresses them at the
price of more false alarms, whose workload \S\ref{sec:analysis} quantifies.
We avoid a fixed ``recall $\ge$ 95\%'' rule: with 20 val anomalies per object it is decided by the
single hardest one.
Other rules are compared in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Tier~0 predicts every image as normal.
Tier~1 scores the mean absolute pixel difference to the mean train-normal image of each object, on
un-normalised RGB.
Tier~3 is PatchCore \citep{roth2022towards}: layer2 and layer3 features averaged over $3 \times 3$
neighbourhoods (1,536 channels), a 1\% greedy coreset of train patches selected in a 128-d random
projection, and the distance to the nearest bank entry as patch score, maximised over the image.
Tiers~2 and~3 share backbone and input; all tiers use the same best-F2 threshold rule.
