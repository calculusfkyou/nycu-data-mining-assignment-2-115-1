\paragraph{Pipeline.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the Tier~2 pipeline, which follows PaDiM \citep{defard2021padim}.
After the preprocessing of \S\ref{sec:data}, a frozen ImageNet-pretrained WideResNet-50
\citep{zagoruyko2016wide} produces layer2 ($512 \times 28 \times 28$), layer3
($1024 \times 14 \times 14$) and layer4 ($2048 \times 7 \times 7$) maps.
Layer3 and layer4 are bilinearly upsampled to $28 \times 28$ and concatenated, giving a
3,584-dimensional feature $f_{ij}(x)$ at each of the 784 locations.
We keep a random subset of $d = 200$ channels, drawn once per seed and shared by train and test images
(\S\ref{sec:eda} showed the features are highly redundant).
The layers and $d$ were chosen on val PR-AUC (\S\ref{sec:setup}); no parameter is fitted on test data.

\paragraph{Anomaly score.}
For every location $(i,j)$ we fit a Gaussian with mean $\mu_{ij}$ and covariance $\Sigma_{ij}$ on the
train normals.
The location score is the Mahalanobis distance, and the image score is the maximum over locations:
\begin{align}
  s_{ij}(x) &= \sqrt{(f_{ij} - \mu_{ij})^\top (\Sigma_{ij} + \epsilon I)^{-1} (f_{ij} - \mu_{ij})},
  \label{eq:sij} \\
  s(x) &= \max_{i,j}\, s_{ij}(x), \label{eq:sx}
\end{align}
with $\epsilon = 0.01$ to keep $\Sigma_{ij}$ invertible.
We use the maximum because the median defect covers 0.20\% of the image (\S\ref{sec:data}), about 1.6
of the 784 locations, so any averaging over the map would dilute it.
The map $s_{ij}(x)$ also serves as the explanation of each alert (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\paragraph{Threshold decision.}
An alert is raised when $s(x) \ge \tau$.
For each object, $\tau$ maximises $F_2 = 5PR / (4P + R)$ on val, where $P$ and $R$ are precision and
recall.
$F_2$ weights recall more heavily than precision, which matches the cost asymmetry below.
We do not use a fixed rule such as ``recall $\ge$ 95\%'': with 20 val anomalies per object, that rule
is decided by the single hardest val anomaly and is therefore unstable.
Alternatives (best-F1, the 99th percentile of val normals, and a review budget) are compared in
\S\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{FP vs.\ FN cost.}
A false negative ships a defective product to a customer, which leads to returns and possibly a safety
risk.
A false positive sends a good product to manual re-inspection, which costs labour.
The false negative is more expensive, so our $\tau$ is set to suppress false negatives at the cost of
more false alarms.
\S\ref{sec:analysis} converts the resulting false alarms into inspection workload.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Tier~0 predicts every image as normal.
Tier~1 uses the mean train-normal image of each object as a template and scores the mean absolute pixel
difference, on un-normalised RGB inputs.
Tier~3 is PatchCore \citep{roth2022towards}.
It averages layer2 and layer3 features over $3 \times 3$ neighbourhoods (1,536 channels, no random
subset) and keeps a 1\% greedy coreset of the train patches as a memory bank, selected in a 128-d random
projection.
A patch is scored by its distance to the nearest bank entry, and the image score is again the maximum.
Tiers~2 and~3 share the backbone and the input; Tier~1 shares only the input size.
All tiers use the same per-object best-F2 threshold rule.
