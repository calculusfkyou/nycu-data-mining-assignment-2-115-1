\section{Exploratory Feature Analysis}
\label{sec:eda}

All statistics in this section use train normals and val images only; test images are never read.
Each image is represented by the spatially averaged layer2 and layer3 activations of an
ImageNet-pretrained \citep{deng2009imagenet} WideResNet-50 \citep{zagoruyko2016wide}, giving 1,536
dimensions.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{stage4_eda/fig_eda_paper.png}
  \caption{Normal vs.\ anomalous images in the global embedding, for one object per pattern.
    Top: PCA fitted on train normals (axes are unitless projections). Bottom: Mahalanobis distance to the
    train-normal Gaussian, val normal vs.\ val anomaly; KS is the two-sample statistic. All 12 objects are
    plotted in the repository (\texttt{results/stage4\_eda}).}
  \label{fig:eda}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Distribution contrast.}
In the PCA projection (Figure~\ref{fig:eda}, top), val anomalies fall inside the train-normal cloud for
most objects, so a single global embedding is a weak detector.
Against a Ledoit--Wolf Gaussian \citep{ledoit2004well} fitted on train normals (bottom), the two-sample
KS statistic between the Mahalanobis distances of val normals and val anomalies is 0.66--0.91 for ten
objects, but only 0.45 for macaroni2 and 0.30 for capsules ($p = 0.12$, not significant); both contain
several freely placed instances.

\paragraph{Dispersion and redundancy.}
Across train normals, the per-location variance of layer3 features is 10.4 for capsules and 7.8 for
macaroni2 but 1.3--3.6 elsewhere: moving instances break the location-wise Gaussian assumption.
The features are also redundant: layer2 and layer3 have a linear CKA \citep{kornblith2019similarity}
of 0.68--0.97, and 109--287 of 1,536 pooled dimensions explain 95\% of the variance, which is why
Tier~2 keeps a random channel subset (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Acquisition shortcut.}
For three objects the val anomalies concentrate in the smaller of two $k$-means modes of the train
normals: cashew (35.8\% of normals vs.\ 95.0\% of anomalies), pcb1 (20.1\% vs.\ 95.0\%; the minor PCA
cluster in Figure~\ref{fig:eda}) and pcb2 (23.2\% vs.\ 70.0\%).
Anomalous cashew and pcb2 images are brighter (mean grey 62.7 vs.\ 53.1 / 55.8 in the two modes, and
97.7 vs.\ 86.8 / 91.0), and 45\% of anomalous pcb1 images are rotated by 180\textdegree\ (normals:
about 7\%), so a detector can score well without seeing the defect.
We flagged objects with a gap of at least 40 percentage points before any test result and also report
every main result without them.

\paragraph{Hypotheses} (revisited in \S\ref{sec:results} and \S\ref{sec:analysis}).
\textbf{H1:} Tier~2 loses most to Tier~3 on objects with high positional variance (capsules,
macaroni2).
\textbf{H2:} small defects are diluted in global features, so patch-level scoring is needed.
\textbf{H3:} max aggregation of the anomaly map is more sensitive to small defects than a top-1\% mean.
