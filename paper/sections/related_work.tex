\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Benchmarks for image anomaly detection.}
Our task falls in the image data category.
Industrial inspection is usually studied on MVTec AD \citep{bergmann2019mvtec}, 15 categories and 5,354
images in which a model is fitted on defect-free images and tested on a mix of normal and defective ones.
Image-level AUROC on MVTec AD is now close to saturated (Table~\ref{tab:related}).
VisA \citep{zou2022spot} keeps the same one-class protocol but adds objects with complex structure (PCBs)
and with multiple, freely placed instances (capsules, candles, macaroni).
The same PatchCore configuration that reaches 99.8 AUROC on MVTec AD reaches 92.4 on VisA in the authors'
own evaluation \citep{zou2022spot}, so VisA still separates methods and is the dataset we use.

\paragraph{Method families.}
\citet{ruff2021unifying} group deep and shallow detectors into density estimation, one-class
classification and reconstruction models.
On industrial images, the strongest normal-only detectors combine ImageNet-pretrained CNN features with a
simple model of normality.
SPADE \citep{cohen2020spade} uses $k$-nearest-neighbour distances to normal images and normal patches.
\citet{rippel2021modeling} fit one multivariate Gaussian to global features and score images by
Mahalanobis distance.
PaDiM \citep{defard2021padim} fits one Gaussian per spatial location of a patch-feature map, which makes
the score a location-wise Mahalanobis distance.
PatchCore \citep{roth2022towards} replaces the Gaussian with a coreset-subsampled memory bank of normal
patch features and scores the nearest-neighbour distance.
Table~\ref{tab:related} lists data, method family, paradigm and the reported image-level result of
each work.

\paragraph{Positioning and choice of tiers.}
The three tiers are ordered by how much each assumes about normal images.
Tier~1 (template difference) assumes that normal images are pixel-aligned with the class-mean image, so
any deviation from that mean is a defect.
This assumption fails for objects that move or come in several instances, which is why Tier~1 is a lower
bound and not a competitor.
Tier~2 (PaDiM) moves the comparison into pretrained feature space but keeps a weaker form of alignment:
each location must follow a single Gaussian.
It is cheap to fit, has one interpretable score per location, and has few hyperparameters to ablate,
which makes it suitable as our primary method.
Tier~3 (PatchCore) drops both the Gaussian and the alignment assumption by comparing each patch with its
nearest normal patch anywhere in the image, at the cost of a memory bank and a nearest-neighbour search.
Tiers~2 and~3 share the backbone and the input, so their gap isolates the normality model.

\paragraph{What we do differently.}
Prior work on VisA reports threshold-free AUROC and AU-PR on the official test set, where about half of
the images are anomalous.
Instead, we fix one stratified split with a labelled validation set, choose one threshold per object
on it, and report the resulting operating point at a deployment prevalence of 1\% and 5\%.
We also report variation over seeds, ablate choices specific to anomaly detection, and audit the data
itself.
For cashew, pcb1 and pcb2 the anomalies differ from the normals in acquisition conditions, so we also
report results without these three objects (\S\ref{sec:eda}).
