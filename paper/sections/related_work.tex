\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Benchmarks and methods.}
Our task falls in the image data category.
Industrial inspection is usually studied on MVTec AD \citep{bergmann2019mvtec} (15 categories, 5,354
images), where image-level AUROC is now close to saturated (Table~\ref{tab:related}).
VisA \citep{zou2022spot} keeps the one-class protocol but adds complex structures (PCBs) and multiple,
freely placed instances; the same PatchCore configuration drops from 99.8 AUROC on MVTec AD to 92.4 on
VisA \citep{zou2022spot}, so VisA still separates methods.
Of the families in the review by \citet{ruff2021unifying}, the strongest normal-only detectors for this
data combine ImageNet-pretrained CNN features with a simple density or nearest-neighbour model: a global
Gaussian \citep{rippel2021modeling}, a Gaussian per location (PaDiM, \citealp{defard2021padim}), or
$k$NN to normal patches (SPADE, \citealp{cohen2020spade}; PatchCore with a coreset memory bank,
\citealp{roth2022towards}).
Table~\ref{tab:related} lists the data, family, paradigm and reported result of each.

\paragraph{Choice of tiers.}
The tiers are ordered by what they assume about normal images.
Tier~1 (template difference) assumes pixel alignment with the class-mean image, which fails for objects
that move, so it is a lower bound.
Tier~2 (PaDiM) compares in pretrained feature space but still assumes one Gaussian per location; it is
cheap, gives an interpretable score per location and has few hyperparameters, so it is our primary
method.
Tier~3 (PatchCore) drops both assumptions by matching each patch to its nearest normal patch anywhere,
at the cost of a memory bank and a nearest-neighbour search.
Tiers~2 and~3 share backbone and input, so their gap isolates the normality model.

\paragraph{What we do differently.}
Prior VisA work reports threshold-free AUROC and AU-PR on the official test set, 55\% of which is
anomalous.
We instead choose one threshold per object on a labelled validation split and report the operating
point at a deployment prevalence of 1\% and 5\%, with variation over seeds, anomaly-specific ablations
and an audit of the data that flags an acquisition shortcut in three objects (\S\ref{sec:eda}).
