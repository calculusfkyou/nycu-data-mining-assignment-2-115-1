\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Scenario.}
On a production line every item is photographed, and quality-control staff must decide whether it ships.
Our detector serves these inspectors: it scores each image, and an alert ($s(x) \ge \tau$) rejects the
item or sends it to manual re-inspection.
A missed defect reaches a customer; a false alarm costs inspection labour, so the threshold must trade
the two at the real defect rate of the line, around 1\%.

\paragraph{Motivation and anomaly definition.}
This is an image anomaly detection task.
An anomaly is an item with a surface or structural defect, such as a bent pin on an otherwise normal
circuit board (pcb1 in Figure~\ref{fig:samples}).
The task is hard for three reasons.
Defects are rare and varied, so few labelled examples exist and models must learn from normal images.
They are tiny, with a median area of 0.20\% of the image.
And on some objects the normal appearance itself varies, because several instances lie at random
positions.
We study these issues on VisA \citep{zou2022spot}, 12 objects photographed under inspection conditions.

\paragraph{Research question.}
How well does a location-wise Gaussian on pretrained patch features (PaDiM, our Tier~2) detect defects
when it is fitted on normal images only, how far is it from a strong reference (PatchCore, Tier~3), and
what would its alerts cost an inspection team at a 1\% defect rate?

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A leakage-free comparison of three tiers on one shared VisA split with per-object thresholds:
    Tier~3 beats Tier~2 by $+0.084$ PR-AUC (significant over seeds and objects), yet at a 1\% defect
    rate only 3.7\% of Tier~2 and 14.8\% of Tier~3 alerts are real defects (\S\ref{sec:results}).
  \item Controlled ablations showing that the location-wise covariance carries Tier~2 ($-0.07$ to
    $-0.12$ PR-AUC without it), while up to 2\% training contamination and the aggregation rule have no
    effect beyond seed noise (\S\ref{sec:results}).
  \item An error and interpretability analysis that traces Tier~2's failures to position: its map peaks
    inside the defect for 25\% of anomalies, against 70\% for Tier~3 (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).
  \item A data audit that finds an acquisition shortcut in three objects before any test result; every
    main result is also reported without them (\S\ref{sec:eda}).
\end{itemize}
