\section{Conclusion \& Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Key findings.}
Fitted on normal images only, PaDiM (Tier~2) reaches 0.801 PR-AUC on VisA, significantly below
PatchCore (0.885).
Its location-wise Gaussian is both its strength (up to 0.12 PR-AUC) and its blind spot (shifted
objects, a quarter of defects localised).
At a 1\% defect rate neither tier can reject items automatically: only 2--5\% of flagged items are
defective.

\paragraph{Reflection and future work.}
The decision that mattered most was the threshold rule, which alone moves the share of real defects
among alerts from 2.4\% to 13.8\% (recall 0.82 to 0.29); starting again, we would fix the inspection
budget with the stakeholder first.
Following \S\ref{sec:analysis}, we would next (1) register each image to a reference pose before Tier~2
scoring and (2) let each patch be compared with the Gaussians of its spatial neighbours, removing the
position blind spot at Tier~2 cost.

\paragraph{Limitations and deployment.}
Each object comes from one acquisition setup and our test set is 43\% anomalous, so absolute numbers
are optimistic, more so for cashew, pcb1 and pcb2; with 50--101 test normals per object, FPR is
resolved only to 1--2\%.
We assume clean normal training data (tested up to 2\% contamination) and roughly aligned objects, which
fails for capsules; our split differs from the official one, so results may not transfer exactly.
In deployment, changes in lighting, camera position or product revision shift the normal appearance:
refitting Tier~2 takes seconds, but $\tau$ needs new labelled anomalies, so the alert rate should be
monitored for drift.
Both tiers run in 7--12\,ms per item on one GPU, and the images contain products, not personal data.
