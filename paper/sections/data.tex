\section{Task Definition \& Data}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Anomaly definition.}
The unit of detection is one image of one product, which is \emph{normal} if the object meets
specification and \emph{anomalous} if it shows a surface defect (scratch, burn, colour spot, crack,
bubble) or a structural one (bent, misplaced or missing part); see Figure~\ref{fig:samples}.
The median defect covers only 0.20\% of the image (10th / 90th percentile 0.03\% / 1.67\%).

\paragraph{Dataset and labels.}
We use VisA \citep{zou2022spot} (CC BY 4.0, \url{https://github.com/amazon-science/spot-diff}):
$N = 10{,}821$ images, 9,621 normal and 1,200 anomalous, of 12 objects with 34 defect types in three
groups: complex structure (pcb1--4), multiple instances (candle, capsules, macaroni1--2) and single
instance (cashew, chewing gum, fryum, pipe fryum).
Every tier receives a $224 \times 224 \times 3$ input ($D = 150{,}528$).
Labels and pixel masks come from the dataset authors (masks used only for analysis); subtle defects may
carry unquantified label noise, and all capsules anomalies share one label (bubble).
The setting is semi-supervised: models fit normal images only, and a few labelled anomalies only choose
thresholds and hyperparameters.

\paragraph{Split and preprocessing.}
We fix one split (seed~0) for all tiers (Table~\ref{tab:data}).
Images are resized directly to $224 \times 224$ and normalised with ImageNet statistics.
We avoid the usual resize-256 plus centre-crop-224, which on val masks would remove 4.1\% of defect
pixels on average and 22.9\% for macaroni2.

\paragraph{Class imbalance.}
The anomaly : normal ratio is 1 : 8.02 overall (11.1\%), 1 : 3.98 in val and 1 : 1.34 in test, all
far above a production rate.
Predicting all normal gives 57.2\% test accuracy and 99.0\% at a 1\% prevalence while catching nothing,
so we never report accuracy; plain cross-entropy training would drift to the same rule.
We instead fit on normal images only, use PR-AUC, which depends on the anomaly rate
\citep{davis2006relationship,saito2015precision}, as the primary metric, and convert each operating
point to the precision at a deployment prevalence $\pi \in \{1\%, 5\%\}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{prec}_\pi = \frac{\pi \cdot \mathrm{TPR}}{\pi \cdot \mathrm{TPR} + (1-\pi) \cdot \mathrm{FPR}}.
  \label{eq:prec-pi}
\end{equation}
